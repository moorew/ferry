0.2.0 and keeps your history.

## Security fixes

These come from a security review of 0.2.0. None of them let anyone outside your own
machine or your own devices read your clipboard.

- **Other programs on the same computer could sync your history** (high). TailCopy
  trusted connections from the computer's own Tailscale address as if they were one of
  your devices, so another user account or a sandboxed app on that computer could read
  your clipboard history. TailCopy now refuses them.
- **Send and Receive switches could be bypassed** by a device that came back with a new
  device id, for example after restoring a backup. The switches now follow the device
  on your tailnet, whatever id it uses.
- **Turning on "Only my devices"** now disconnects relays and other peers that were
  allowed before, instead of leaving them connected until they dropped.
- **Image size limit:** a device could send a picture larger than your limit by
  understating its size. Downloads now stop at the limit.
- **Deleted clips** no longer leave a fingerprint of their text in the history
  database, which could have revealed short deleted items like PINs. Existing ones are
  cleaned up the first time 0.2.1 starts.
- **Linux command line:** other user accounts on the same computer could send commands
  to TailCopy (pause it, or paste) or stop it from starting. Its socket is now private to
  you on every system.
- **A mistake in config.toml** no longer quietly turns off your privacy rules. TailCopy
  keeps a copy of the file as `config.toml.bad`, pauses capture and tells you.

## Other hardening

- History, settings and logs are stored in folders only your account can open.
- Links inside copied web content can't open other pages inside TailCopy's windows.
- Copying something while capture is paused is never overwritten by a clip arriving
  from another device.
- Images put on the Windows clipboard use a standard bitmap header.
- Release builds pin every build tool to an exact version and only build from tagged
  commits on `main`.

## Known issues

On Ubuntu, clips that arrive from another device are mirrored as plain text or an
image; paste them from the picker to keep their formatting. macOS has now been tested on
a real Mac.
